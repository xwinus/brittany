Design language · brittany

Gwenn ha Du

White and black. The Breton flag is nine horizontal stripes — and horizontal stripes of uneven length are exactly what brittany leaves behind in a source file. The project's name hands us a visual system for free; the work is to translate it into the vocabulary of a formatter rather than copy it: lines, a column, alignment.

Where it comes from

The README states three goals that can be drawn: be clever about using the available horizontal space without overflowing the column maximum, align things horizontally, and be idempotent. Those are properties, not features — and properties are what a mark can carry.

The name supplies the rest. Gwenn ha Du, the Breton flag, is nine horizontal black-and-white stripes: very nearly a block of code, and unclaimed in developer tooling.

What follows is two halves of one system rather than two competing options. A is the mark, B is the wordmark. They are used together and apart.

A

The column

Five bars flush to the left edge, each a different length, like lines of code. A thin vertical rule sits on the right: the column maximum. No bar crosses it; the longest comes right up to it. That is the whole of brittany in one image — use the horizontal space, never overflow.

Bars and gaps are close to equal in height, so the mark also reads as a fragment of flag. White is material here, not background, which is why the mark is never drawn inside a box or a rounded square.

The profile is deliberate, not arbitrary. The third bar is a waist; the fourth is the longest and carries the accent, which puts the mark's weight low and makes the bar that reaches the limit the last thing the eye meets. The top and bottom bars differ by three units so that no axis of symmetry appears — symmetry would read as ornament rather than as source.

B

The wordmark

Always lowercase — that is how the tool is invoked. Set in monospace (IBM Plex Mono, weight 500), with a thin vertical rule in the accent colour immediately after the last letter: the column limit again, this time reading as a cursor.

It is the cheapest thing to deploy. It works in the README heading, in --help output, in the Hackage description. It needs no artwork, only a rule: never a capital B, never a family other than the mono, and the vertical rule always trails.

Minimum size 11 px. Below that the accent rule stops resolving and should be dropped rather than scaled further.

Colour

Monochrome ground, one yellow strike

The palette comes from Breton material rather than from preference: granite, slate, deep water, and ajonc — the gorse that flowers bright yellow along the coast the whole year round. The flag sets the ratio: the overwhelming majority of any surface is neutral, and colour is rare.

The two accents have separate jobs and never swap. Gorse is purely graphic — the highlighted bar in the mark, the cursor, an active line. Sea is functional — links, focus, interactive state. That separation is what lets the yellow stay saturated: it never has to meet a text contrast threshold.

Typography

Three faces, cleanly divided

RoleFaceUse
Fraunces Fraunces 600 / 400 italic Headings only. Keep them short, never set a paragraph in it. It lends the identity a little heraldic age, which suits the name.
IBM Plex Sans IBM Plex Sans 400 / 500 Body copy, captions, UI. Neutral without being characterless; hold the measure near 65 characters.
IBM Plex Mono IBM Plex Mono 400 / 500 The wordmark, all code, version tags, and small uppercase labels at 0.1em tracking.

Plex Sans and Plex Mono share a skeleton, so moving between prose and code does not jar. Fraunces stands against them deliberately as the outsider, which is exactly why it is confined to headings.

Rules

What holds it together

+Keep one bar pitch of clear space on all four sides.
−Never outline the mark or set it in a rounded square — the gaps are material, not background.
+Colour exactly one bar, always the fourth.
−Never let gorse carry text, an icon stroke, or a border.
+Let the column rule bleed past the mark's top and bottom edge.
−Never re-sort, even out, or re-space the bar widths.
+Drop to the three-bar reduction below 24 px.
−Never set the wordmark with a capital B, or in anything but the mono.
+Keep the flag abstract — principle, not shape.
−Never reproduce the Breton flag itself, ermine canton included.

Still open

Two decisions not yet made

01

Haskell purple, or not.

The official #5E5086 would place the project in its ecosystem instantly, at the cost of blending it into a dozen other tools. Gorse is the riskier and more distinctive call. Recorded here as a deliberate choice, not an oversight.

02

Relationship to lspitzner/brittany.

The upstream project carries no identity, so nothing is being inherited — but it is unsettled whether this one should signal continuity or a restart. That affects the tone of the microsite copy rather than the drawing.
